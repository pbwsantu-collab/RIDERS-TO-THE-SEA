Page 141: Introduction & Dramatis Personae

RIDERS TO THE SEA by JOHN MILLINGTON SYNGE

About the Play: Riders to the Sea is set on an Aran Island. The play centres on the struggle of a family against the sea, exploring fatalism and the power of nature.

Dramatis Personae: Maurya (an old woman), Cathleen (her daughter, about 20), Nora (her daughter, younger), Bartley (her son), and various neighbours.

Page 142: Opening Scene

(An island cottage kitchen. Nets, oil-skins, spinning wheel, new boards. Cathleen, about twenty, kneads cake. Nora enters softly.) NORA: [In a low voice.] Where is she? CATHLEEN: She's lying down, God help her, and may be sleeping, if she's able. (Nora comes in and takes a bundle from under her shawl.) CATHLEEN: [Spinning] What is it you have? NORA: The young priest is after bringing them. It's a shirt and a plain stocking were got off a drowned man in Donegal... NORA: The young priest says he's known the like of it. "If it's Michael's they are," says he, "you can tell herself he's got a clean burial by the grace of God..."

Page 143: The Threatening Sea

CATHLEEN: Is the sea bad by the white rocks, Nora? NORA: Middling bad, God help us. There's a great roaring in the west, and it's worse it'll be getting when the tide's turned to the wind... CATHLEEN: Give me the ladder, and I'll put them up in the turf-loft, the way she won't know of them at all... (A gust of wind blows open the door. Cathleen and Nora react.)

Page 144: Bartley & The Rope

BARTLEY: Where is the bit of new rope, Cathleen, was bought in Connemara? MAURYA: You'd do right to leave that rope, Bartley... It will be wanting in this place if Michael is washed up... BARTLEY: I've no halter the way I can ride down on the mare, and I must go now quickly... MAURYA: Isn't it a hard and cruel man won't hear a word from an old woman, and she holding him from the sea? (Bartley leaves without Maurya's blessing.)

Page 145: Searching for Michael

CATHLEEN: We've looked for Michael nine days, and the sea is still roaring. NORA: The young priest said he'd be washed up by the grace of God. (Nora and Cathleen examine the stocking – it matches Michael's.)

Page 146: Bartley Prepares to Leave

BARTLEY: [Putting on a new coat] I must go now. The mare is ready. MAURYA: Take the bread and my blessing, for the sea is cruel. (Maurya goes to the spring well to bless him.)

Page 147–148: Maurya's Lament

MAURYA: [At the well] I've seen Michael himself... riding on the gray pony, with fine clothes on him. CATHLEEN: [Keen] It's destroyed we are from this day. (Men carry in Bartley's body – he was knocked into the sea by the gray pony.)

Pages 149–151: Climax & Acceptance

MAURYA: [Sprinkling holy water] They're all gone now. There isn't anything more the sea can do to me. CATHLEEN: What more can we do? MAURYA: No man at all can be living for ever, and we must be satisfied. (She kneels and prays. The play ends with quiet resignation.)

Comprehensive Literary & Linguistic Analysis

1. Theme of Fatalism & Pagan-Christian Syncretism

Maurya's world is governed by oceanic fatalism. Christian symbols are present, but the underlying worldview is pagan. The sea acts as a tyrannical deity demanding tragic tribute.

2. Symbolic Elements

  • The Red Mare & Gray Pony: The gray pony evokes the pale horse of Death.
  • The Rope: Intended for horses, becomes designated for Michael's coffin.
  • White Boards: Bought for Michael's coffin, used for Bartley.

3. Dialect & Syntax

Hiberno-English syntax (e.g., "is after bringing") gives the play its distinctive poetic rhythm.
